# R7a and R7b: our challenge families as the training distribution, and our guards in the training loop

Plan `RESEARCH-LEARNED-AVOIDANCE.md` (robotics-lab), section 4, the R7 table. Both parts are one task,
`Mjlab-Nav-Ours-MicroDuck`: the deploy task (`docs/M2.md`: a 10 Hz policy, lateral 0) whose scenes come from robotics-lab's
challenge generator and whose command passes through the navigator's reflex and cliff stop before the walker. Judged against M2b
(NVIDIA's recipe, deploy-matched, 400 iterations) on `avoidance_v1`, which training never sees.

Everything here ran on CPU. Nothing has run on a GPU, at 4096 envs, or on a trained model (section 8).

## 1. R7a: the pool

**The file.** `lab-scenarios pool` (robotics-lab, `libs/runner`, `labrunner.scenarios`) writes the items of `export` one per line,
in the order of the challenges given and the seeds ascending, and `FILE.manifest.json` beside it: the file's sha256, the commit,
the command, the counts by challenge, `max_obstacles`, and what each `--exclude-dataset` removed.

```bash
cd robotics-lab/libs/runner && uv run lab-scenarios pool --challenges 1,2,3,4,14,15 --seeds 1000-5999 \
  --exclude-dataset /work/robotics-evals/data/sources/avoidance-v1/manifest.json \
  --out /work/microduck-nav/data/pools/ours-v1.jsonl
```

`ours-v1`: 30,000 items, 5,000 of each of corridor (1), doorway (2), corner_in_doorway (3), box_in_open (4), thin_legs (14) and
small_boxes (15). 29,722,953 bytes, sha256 `b34cc964f8b0...` (`data/pools/ours-v1.manifest.json`, committed). The file is **not**
committed: it is over the 20 MB limit (it gzips to 2.2 MB, and the loader reads `.jsonl.gz`; committing that is the operator's
call). `data/pools/*.jsonl` is git-ignored; the file sits in this checkout's working tree on the machine that made it.

- `avoidance_v1` is seeds 1-5 of the same six challenges and the pool's seeds are 1000-5999, so nothing is excluded by the
  manifest (`excluded: 0`); `--exclude-dataset` is still passed, so the day the ranges meet the command refuses, not the reader.
- **Every pool must exclude both evaluation sets, `avoidance_v1` and `avoidance_v2`** (repeat `--exclude-dataset`). v2 is being
  built with new challenge numbers, so it cannot overlap `ours-v1`, but its manifest did not exist when this was written
  (`/work/robotics-evals/data/sources/avoidance-v2/manifest.json`). **Step for tomorrow:** when it exists, run the command above
  with both `--exclude-dataset` lines; the bytes must be unchanged (sha256 above) and the manifest then lists both sets: commit it.
- The pool's manifest names robotics-lab commit `224e7d5d3c5e`, `dirty: true`: the `pool` command and its test
  (`libs/runner/tests/test_pool.py`) are uncommitted in robotics-lab's working tree, and the generator (`challenges.py`) is that
  commit's. Until `pool` is committed there, the file can only be made on this machine: copy it, and check the sha256.

**The loader** (`microduck_nav/pool.py`). Each item is read once and moved into the trunk's frame at the start: the duck's start
frame (the item's `start`) with the trunk 0.05 m ahead of it (`labrunner.scene.TRUNK_START`). Start and goal come from the item in
the robot's reset frame; at reset the scene is placed by the robot's own pose, so the item is where it should be relative to the
robot whatever its random heading (`reset_base` draws yaw over a full turn). A line that is not an item, or a number that is not
finite, is an error with its file and line.

**The slots: raise the count, and size them per environment.** NVIDIA's eight slots are fixed shapes (a 0.9 m wall, a 0.4 x 0.3
box, two 11 cm posts). The pool's scenes do not fit them by shape even where they fit by count (22,500 of the 30,000 items have
eight obstacles or fewer, none with the right shapes): a corridor is 4 walls of 3.8 and 0.8 m, a doorway room 2.4 to 3.6 m a
side, a chair's leg 3 cm across, a field of small boxes 8 to 20 cm. Tiling 0.9 m walls would need about 18 slots for one doorway
and 12 for the corridor, 0.12 m thick instead of 0.04, and could not make a 3 cm leg at all. So:

- The Ours task has **14 pool slots** (`pool_00` to `pool_13`) instead of NVIDIA's eight (`obstacles.py`). 14 is the most any
  item of `ours-v1` holds (small_boxes: 4 room walls and 6 to 10 boxes); `max_obstacles` is in the manifest and a test checks the
  two agree. Obstacles per family: corridor 4, doorway 6, corner_in_doorway 7, box_in_open 5, thin_legs 5 or 9, small_boxes 10 to 14.
- A slot is a unit mocap box **resized per environment at reset**: the item's half sides and height go into the world's
  `geom_size` (and the broadphase's `geom_rbound`, `geom_aabb`, as mjlab's `dr.geom_size` keeps them), the pose is the box's
  centre. A box is the exact shape: robotics-lab's simulator builds every obstacle as a box geom, so no cylinder slot is needed and
  `kind` (wall, box, leg, small, seat) is only counted. The format has no other shape; a new one needs a loader change, not a silent box.
- A slot the item does not use is parked 30 m from its environment's origin, a box nothing reaches.
- **An item with more obstacles than slots is never cut**: the loader skips it and counts it (`stats.skipped_too_many`, printed
  at the first reset: `pool: ... skipped for more obstacles than slots: N`). For `ours-v1` that count is 0 (printed:
  `pool: 30000 items read, 25000 kept; held out: 5000 doorway; skipped for more obstacles than slots: 0; most obstacles in an item: 14`).
  A pool of mined scenes (R7c) with a larger item meets the skip and its count, or `NavOptions.pool_slots` is raised.
- The rewards' geometry (`field.py`, `nav_math.py`) takes half sizes per environment. NVIDIA's tasks keep their static `(k, 2)`
  and are unchanged; tests run NVIDIA's own reward functions against ours with per-environment sizes (`test_r7a_geometry.py`).
- A box whose underside is over the duck (thin_legs' seat, 0.42 m up: 2,500 of the 30,000 items) is a real box for the rays and
  the contacts, and is **not** in the rewards' planar footprints: under it the duck is not inside an obstacle.

**The held-out family.** `holdout_family` (default `doorway`) leaves a layout out of training: 5,000 items out, 25,000 in. It is
`NavOptions.holdout_family` and a command-line option of `train`, comma separated, `none` for nothing:

```bash
--env.events.obstacle-field.params.holdout-family doorway               # the default
--env.events.obstacle-field.params.holdout-family doorway,corner_in_doorway
--env.events.obstacle-field.params.holdout-family none
```

A name that is not a layout of the file is an error (a typo does not train on everything). **corner_in_doorway has a doorway in
it** (the same room, a 0.6 m gap, and a box just inside), so the default holds out the doorway family but not every door frame:
a second run with `doorway,corner_in_doorway` is the clean generalisation test, and its cost is the operator's call.

**The command** (`PoolNavigationCommand`): the same request law as NVIDIA's destination command (speed 0.2 to 0.35 m/s,
heading gain 1.5, stop within 0.2 m), toward the item's goal. It is a straight request toward the goal, not the follower's path;
how far the two differ in our families is not measured.

## 2. R7b: the guards in the loop

**Where.** `guards.py`, between the policy's command and the walker, the order of robotics-lab's navigator
(`robots/duck/src/duck_lab/nav/run.py`, commit `224e7d5` of its mission-control branch, whose `run.py` is that of `d794a45`):
`_control` (1242) makes the policy's command, the cliff stop (1263-1280) and the reflex stop (1337-1360) may replace it with
`Twist()`, `move` (1376) sends it. In the action term: the residual forms the command (the policy's), `GuardLayer` may replace it
(`action.py` `_guard`), the walker gets the result.

**The rule, constant by constant** (profile `robots/duck/profile.json`):

| | rule | where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reflex | `vx > 0` and (`block < reflex_ahead_m` 0.30 or `near < emergency_mm` 150) | run.py:1337-1338 |
| block | nearest valid return that is an obstacle (not below the floor by `extra_m` 0.06, not floor: under `floor_margin_m` 0.08 + 0.03 per m of horizontal range, not over `overhead_clear_m` 0.30), ahead of the trunk (> 0) and within `radius_m` 0.083 + `reflex_margin_m` 0.04 = 0.123 m of its heading | tof8x8.py:280-319 |
| near | the nearest valid return in the four central columns (2 to 5) of the 8 rows | tof8x8.py:192-195 |
| cliff | `vx > 0`, drop points seen in 3 new frames in a row (`DROP_FRAMES`), the nearest drop under `cliff_stop_m` 0.60 | run.py:1248-1254, 1263 |
| drop point | a beam at least 0.15 down whose floor point is within `look_m` 0.7 horizontally and within `corridor_m` 0.25 of the heading, where the zone returned nothing or a return at or past `height + extra_m` along the down axis; at least `min_zones` 2 of them | tof8x8.py:326-359 |

What a fired guard sends is `Twist()`: **vx and vyaw both 0** (run.py:1264 and 1340), so the whole command is zeroed (vy is 0
in this task anyway), not only the forward speed. The cliff is checked first and takes the tick (run.py:1263 `continue`s).

Differences from the navigator, each deliberate:

- **Geometry is the sensor's true pose and beams** (the episode's mount pitch and height draw included): a calibrated navigator.
  The navigator's real chain (IMU quaternion, odometry height, `pitch_offset_deg`) is not reproduced, and with it a mis-calibrated
  mount (the profile notes 5 degrees of pitch error read a false drop). The guard here is never fooled by the mount.
- **The frame is the delivered one**: what the policy reads, with its latency and dropped frames; a frame is "new" when its
  capture time changes, so the streak counts frames, not ticks (run.py:1248).
- **A blanked zone is no drop** only with the impairments on. The navigator skips a zone its impairment blanked (tof8x8.py:347);
  the env cannot tell a blanked zone from an empty one, so with impairments on every invalid zone is skipped, and off every one
  counts. The training floor is flat, so a drop needs a return past the floor line: **the cliff did not fire once in 1,920
  env-ticks** (32 envs, 60 ticks, zero residual, the real pool); it is there for parity.
- **The stop lasts one tick.** After a stop the navigator surveys, remembers the points, faces the path and replans (run.py:1264-1280,
  1341-1359), seconds of standing. Here the policy's next command comes the next tick. Its other refusals (`_arc_clear` 1281-1300,
  `step_look` 1320-1336, stuck 1361-1375) are not ported.

**The applied command is post-guard.** The guard's output replaces `residual.previous` (`action.py` `_guard`), which is what the
`applied_command` observation reads (obs slots 50 to 53), what `command_rate` compares, and where the slew starts next tick, as
learned.py `_command` does (`self.residual.previous = app`, app what the navigator last sent). The observation layout is unchanged:
`tests/test_obs_parity.py` and `test_deploy_options.py` compare the committed fixtures with what the code builds now and pass; the
fixtures were not regenerated, because the observation functions are unchanged and the fixture's applied commands are constructed.

**The cost.** `guard_intervention` (`rewards.py`): 1 for each policy tick at which a guard changed the command, as an event
(divided by `step_dt` so the manager's scaling gives exactly the weight, as `failure` does). Weight **-2.0**
(`env_cfg.GUARD_INTERVENTION_WEIGHT`). **It is tunable and nothing measured sets it**: for scale, `failure` is -30 and `approach`
-4, and a guard that fires every tick of a 200-tick episode costs -400. It adds to `command_rate`, which also sees the jump to 0.
On the zero-residual policy 99 of 1,920 env-ticks (5.2 %) fire the reflex.

**The switch.** `NavOptions.guards` and `--env.actions.nav.guards True|False`. Off, there is no `GuardLayer` and no cost
term (the task's reward list is then NVIDIA's eight). `Mjlab-Nav-Ours-MicroDuck` has it on. If the flag is given `False` on the
command line the cost term is still in the list and is always 0.

the slots resized and placed per environment, the sensor sees the resized box where the item put it, a robot walking into it ends

`tests/test_c7_ours.py` (the real `train` on CPU).

## 5. C7 on CPU

`train Mjlab-Nav-Ours-MicroDuck --gpu-ids None --env.scene.num-envs 8 --agent.max-iterations 10 --agent.save-interval 5
--agent.logger tensorboard`, on the real pool: finished 10 of 10 iterations, every scalar finite, checkpoints `model_0, 5, 9`,
each with its ONNX; the last ONNX is `(1, 1343) -> (1, 3)` and finite (`test_c7_ours.py`, on the committed sample pool: 5 passed,
final code).

CPU time, 8 envs, 24 steps = 120 walker steps per iteration, on a shared 32-core machine with a load average of 10 to 18:

| run | iteration time |
| --- | --- |
| real pool, `train` by hand, 10 iterations | 14.6 s first; 8.7 to 12.1 s after, median 10.3 s; 2 min wall in all |
| `test_c7_ours.py`, sample pool, final code | mean 9.2 s, median 9.6 s, max 9.8 s (9 iterations after the first) |
| the same test, an earlier run on a busier machine | mean 15.7 s, median 15.6 s, max 16.6 s |

The ratio to the deploy task is between 0.9 and 1.5 (median to median), and the machine's load swings by as much: not isolated, and not split between the 14 slots and the guards. The GB10
is not measured.

## 6. The DGX command for tomorrow

M2b is 400 iterations of the deploy task: 400 x 24 policy steps x 0.1 s = 960 s of simulation per environment, 4096 environments,
39.3 million policy steps. `Mjlab-Nav-Ours-MicroDuck` has the same policy rate, the same 24 steps per iteration and the same 4096
environments, so the same simulated time is **400 iterations**.

```bash
MICRODUCK_WALKER_ONNX=/path/to/velstand.onnx MICRODUCK_POOL=/path/to/ours-v1.jsonl \
  uv run train Mjlab-Nav-Ours-MicroDuck --env.scene.num-envs 4096 --agent.max-iterations 400 --agent.save-interval 100 --agent.logger tensorboard
```

- First `sha256sum ours-v1.jsonl` must give `b34cc964f8b0...` (the full value is in `data/pools/ours-v1.manifest.json`). The pool is
  not in git: copy it (30 MB, or 2.2 MB gzipped: set `MICRODUCK_POOL` to the `.jsonl.gz`).
- The task prints the pool line at the first reset; it must say `25000 kept; held out: 5000 doorway` and `skipped ... 0`.
- Checkpoints and ONNX land in `logs/rsl_rl/microduck_nav_ours/<timestamp>/`, as M2's.
- Time: not measured on the GB10. By the deploy task's estimate (13 to 19 s an iteration at 4096 envs, `docs/M2.md`) 400 iterations
  take 1.4 to 2.1 hours; the CPU ratio above allows up to 1.5 times that. A guess.
- Options worth a second window, each one flag: the clean holdout `--env.events.obstacle-field.params.holdout-family doorway,corner_in_doorway`;
  R7a alone (the pool, no guards) `--env.actions.nav.guards False`.

## 7. The evaluation plan

R5 on `avoidance_v1` (30 items: the six families, seeds 1 to 5), the navigator's local slot (robotics-lab `learned.py`), the
model of this run against M2b's, each with the navigator's own guards on, as it deploys; the rules arm as the reference. The
score is R5's: success, collisions, minimum clearance, time, stops (reflex and cliff), falls, and per family. Both models read the
same 1343 vector at `policy_step_s 0.1` and send no lateral (their ONNX metadata says so), so `learned.py` runs both unchanged.

- **The held-out family is scored on its own**: the doorway's five items, R7 against M2b, and against R7's own score on the
  families it trained on. Five items cannot separate 3 of 5 from 5 of 5 (a 95 % interval on 5 of 5 starts near 0.57): run each
  item with several sensor-impairment seeds (`export --depth-impairment`) for at least 20 doorway runs per arm before reading it.
- What R7 shows: **the pool and guards together**. It cannot say which of the two helped; that is the `guards False` window.
- Also read the guard counts: a controller that "wins" by never reaching a guard, against one that reaches it and is stopped.
  R7b's claim is that interventions fall; the run's own `Episode_Reward/guard_intervention` is the training-side number, the
  navigator's `reflex_stop` events the evaluation-side.

## 8. Not covered

- No GPU run, no run at 4096 envs, no trained model past 10 CPU iterations: that it learns, or beats M2b, is not shown. Memory and
  speed with 14 slots, a contact sensor each, at 4096 envs are not measured; CPU iteration time is noisy.
- The pool file is not in git (30 MB); the robotics-lab `pool` command and its test are uncommitted in that repository; the
  manifest says `dirty: true`. `avoidance_v2` is not excluded because its manifest does not exist yet.
- Guards: not the navigator's chain of poses and calibration; not the stop's aftermath (survey, replan, turn first) nor its other
  refusals; a one-tick stop; the cliff untested on a real drop in the env (the floor is flat), only on rendered frames; the
  impairment-blanked rule is a choice (section 2). The weight -2 is a guess.
- The request is a straight line to the goal, not the follower's path; a doorway's request often points at the wall. Pollen's
  walker stands still near 0.2 m/s: in one test an environment asked for 0.203 m/s did not move in 300 steps, so the low end of the
  0.2 to 0.35 m/s request range may not walk.
- A box in the pool is the generator's: no floor features (the pool refuses a challenge with a drop or a step), no cables, no
  rounded legs. The wall is 4 cm thick as in robotics-lab, not NVIDIA's 12 cm.
- Obstacle contact ends the episode as in NVIDIA's task; robotics-lab's judge also counts contact, but its `bounds` and
  `drop_margin` rules and its time limits are not in the training episode (20 s, the field's 5 m).
- `holdout_family` leaves out a layout, not a concept: corner_in_doorway still teaches a door frame.
- The decision on thin_legs' seat: it floats at 0.42 to 0.45 m and is physically there; the duck's head height is 0.27 m, so it
  never touches it, and the reward footprints ignore it. Whether the real sensor's overhead returns matter to the policy is not tested.
