# Milestone M2: the full task, two ways to train it

M2 trains the full layout mix and impairments for 1,500 iterations. There are two commands. They differ in what the
trained model is meant to be run by:

| | Task | Policy rate | Lateral residual | Meant for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) | `Mjlab-Nav-MicroDuck` | 50 Hz, a policy step is a walker step | limit 0.12 m/s | NVIDIA-faithful: their recipe as written |
| (b) | `Mjlab-Nav-Deploy-MicroDuck` | 10 Hz, a policy step is five walker steps (0.1 s) | forced to 0 | robotics-lab's duck navigator, which ticks at 10 Hz and sends no lateral velocity |

Everything else is the same in both: layouts, ToF impairments, rewards and weights, terminations, 20 s episodes, the walker
(Pollen's, at 50 Hz in both), the Beta head, the PPO settings except gamma (below).

## The commands

```bash
MICRODUCK_WALKER_ONNX=/path/to/velstand.onnx uv run train Mjlab-Nav-MicroDuck --env.scene.num-envs 4096 --agent.max-iterations 1500 --agent.save-interval 100 --agent.logger tensorboard
```

```bash
MICRODUCK_WALKER_ONNX=/path/to/velstand.onnx uv run train Mjlab-Nav-Deploy-MicroDuck --env.scene.num-envs 4096 --agent.max-iterations 1500 --agent.save-interval 100 --agent.logger tensorboard
```

The flags are M1's (`docs/M1.md`): `--agent.logger tensorboard` because W&B stops without a key, `--agent.save-interval`
because rsl_rl does not save a stopped run. Logs go to `logs/rsl_rl/microduck_nav/<timestamp>/` for (a) and
`logs/rsl_rl/microduck_nav_deploy/<timestamp>/` for (b), each checkpoint with its `model_N.onnx` beside it.

## What the deploy task changes

Two options on `NavOptions`, each a place to change: `policy_decimation` (1 is 50 Hz; the deploy task uses 5) and
`lateral_limit` (0.12; the deploy task uses 0). The existing tasks keep their values and their behaviour.

**The policy rate, and the mechanism.** mjlab 1.3.0 has one decimation, the physics substeps inside one environment step;
it has nothing for a policy that acts less often than the environment steps. Two pieces do it here:

- The action term holds its action for `policy_decimation` walker steps (`action.py`). On the first step of a tick it takes
  the action and forms the command (residual, limits, slew with `dt = 0.1 s`, the stale-frame and zero-request stops), as
  the navigator's `ResidualCommand(dt = tick_s)` does; on the next four it keeps both. The walker runs on every step and
  sees the held command. Rewards and terminations are evaluated on every walker step. Because the term itself holds, any
  caller is right, including `replay` and `play`, which step at 50 Hz.
- `ActionRepeatVecEnv` (`action_repeat.py`, installed by `NavOnPolicyRunner` when the decimation is above 1) is what PPO
  steps. It steps the environment five times with one action, **sums the rewards** (already scaled by `step_dt`, so the sum
  is the same time integral as at 50 Hz), ORs the dones, and returns the last observation. rsl_rl's storage, GAE and
  `num_steps_per_env = 24` therefore count policy steps. A reward after a mid-tick reset is not added to the finished
  episode; the episode statistics of every reset in the tick are merged, weighted by how many environments reset.
- **gamma is 0.998^5 = 0.990** in (b), so the discount per second is the same as (a)'s. lambda stays 0.95 per policy step
  (a longer credit horizon in seconds; not changed).

**What the policy observes** follows what the navigator builds at a 0.1 s tick:

- the ten ToF history entries are one per tick, 0.1 s apart (one second of history; (a) has 0.2 s). `tick_history.py` is the
  ring, pushed on the walker step that completes a tick; a reset environment backfills it on its first push, as mjlab's
  buffer does. The ages are those at each tick.
- the previous action is the action held, clipped to [-1, 1]; the applied command is the one formed at the last tick.
- the joint velocities are one tick (5 walker steps, 0.1 s) late instead of one step. The navigator's are the previous tick's.
  Their noise (+-0.25) is kept. The IMU lag and noise, the encoder bias and the IMU mounting rotation are the walker task's
  randomisation in both tasks; a deployed sensor brings its own.

**The lateral limit** goes into the residual's bound, so the lateral residual is 0, the applied command's lateral is 0, and
the walker is sent vy = 0. `command_rate` (reward) normalises by the nominal (0.35, 0.12, 0.75) in both tasks, since dividing
by a zero limit would be NaN. The Beta head still has three outputs; the lateral one has no effect.

## Iteration time and which iterations compare

At decimation 5 an iteration is 24 policy steps = 120 walker steps = 2.4 s of simulation per environment, against 0.48 s
for (a). The walker, the rays and the physics run on every step, so collection costs about 5 times as much per iteration.
Measured on CPU, 8 envs: **(a) 2.63 s per iteration (C7, 50 iterations); (b) 10.9 s (median 10.4, max 15.9; 20 iterations),
of which 10.8 s collection and 0.07 s the PPO update**: 4.1 times. The GB10 is not measured; scaling its estimate (3.2 to
4.5 s at 4096 envs) by the same ratio gives roughly 13 to 19 s per iteration, 5.5 to 8 hours for 1,500 iterations, against
1.3 to 1.9 hours for (a). Treat that as an estimate.

Comparing the runs:

- The same iteration number is the same number of policy samples (24 x envs) and the same number of PPO updates, but (b)
  has seen 5 times the simulated time. The same wall-clock time gives (b) about a quarter of (a)'s iterations.
- `Train/mean_reward` and the `Episode_Reward/*` terms are comparable in scale (rewards are dt-scaled sums over the same
  episode), but a policy that holds a command five times as long scores differently on `command_rate`: its squared
  command change per tick is 25 times larger and there are a fifth as many ticks, so the same slew-limited manoeuvre costs
  5 times as much in (b). The weight is NVIDIA's and was not changed.
- `Train/mean_episode_length` is in policy steps: 200 for a full 20 s episode in (b), 1000 in (a).
- Do not compare (a)'s iteration N with (b)'s iteration N as "equally trained"; compare the replayed behaviour, at the rate
  each is meant to run.

## Running a trained model at the navigator

For (b) the navigator must run what the model trained on (`robotics-lab`, `robots/duck/src/duck_lab/plugins/learned.py`):

- `observation(..., step_s = 0.1, ...)`: the ten history entries 0.1 s apart. Today `MODEL_STEP_S` is 0.02 (asserted equal
  to the 50 Hz env's step); for a (b) model it must be the tick, 0.1 s. This is the setting that reproduces
  `tests/fixtures/obs_parity_deploy.json`.
- `Learned(..., tick_s = 0.1)`, the default: `ResidualCommand(tick_s)` is then the training's residual with a 0.1 s slew step.
- Lateral: nothing to add; the duck's `Twist` has none, and the model's lateral output has no effect on the command in
  training either. The observation's previous-action slot 35 is the model's lateral output, clipped, as in training.
- the joint velocities are the previous tick's `body.velocities`, as `learned.py` already keeps them.

A (a) model trained at 50 Hz keeps `MODEL_STEP_S = 0.02` and meets a 10 Hz loop it did not train for, as
`RESEARCH-R4-OBS-PARITY.md` describes.

## Not covered

- Nothing here ran on a GPU, nor at 4096 envs; the times above are CPU, 8 envs.
- No (b) model has been trained past 20 iterations, so that it learns, or learns better than (a) at the navigator's
  10 Hz, is not shown.
- The 5-step action is held by the term but the walker's own policy, noise and sensors are not changed: the walker still
  sees 50 Hz proprioception while the nav policy sees it at 10 Hz.
- An environment that resets in the middle of a tick runs on to the end of the tick with a zero held action, so its first
  policy observation is up to 4 walker steps into the episode (the navigator starts a run at a tick).
- `replay` was not run on a (b) policy; it steps at 50 Hz and the action term holds, so it should be right, but that is
  untested.
- Age is arrival, not capture, at the navigator, and training's frame ages include up to 67 ms of transport delay:
  `RESEARCH-R4-OBS-PARITY.md` still applies.
